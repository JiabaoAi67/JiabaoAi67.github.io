Weight reuse, executed depth, and sampling budget in flow-matching TTS

Audio samples Results Page source

Abstract

We study how to organize Transformer depth through recurrence in flow-matching text-to-speech, varying the amount, order, and placement of weight reuse. Seven layouts perform the same 18 block calls per network evaluation under a common training objective and sampler. On Seed-TTS and LibriSpeech-PC, adjacent reuse of nine blocks retains competitive intelligibility, speaker similarity, and predicted speech quality at 32 sampling steps with 47.1% fewer parameters than the unshared baseline. Cyclic reuse of six blocks three times further reduces model size but raises 32-step word error rates relative to nine blocks used twice. At matched parameter counts and executed depth, reuse order and sharing position produce different quality trade-offs. These comparisons also depend on sampling budget: Prefix and Suffix have similar 32-step word error rates, but Suffix is worse by 3.44 and 5.97 percentage points at four steps on the two datasets, respectively. These results show that the organization of recurrent computation affects synthesis quality, and that reuse layouts should be selected for both the sampling budget and the quality dimensions of interest.

Sharing reduces storage while retaining the executed depth. CYCLE and SEQUENCE 9 × 2 each use 47.1% fewer parameters and 34.5% less peak inference allocation than the Baseline. Their inference allocation is therefore 65.5% of the Baseline; all seven models still execute 18 block calls per network evaluation.

The order of reuse changes the trade-off. At 32 steps, SEQUENCE lowers Seed-TTS WER from CYCLE’s 2.60 to 2.03, while UTMOS falls from 3.90 to 3.75. On LibriSpeech-PC, WER rises from 2.14 to 2.26. SEQUENCE has higher SIM-o on both datasets. Parameter count and executed depth are unchanged.
